Who's behind this

Communications professional, photographer, trail runner

Weekdays: a desk, communication files, brands to bring to life. Weekends: a pack, some trails, and that same urge to tell a story.

I'm not a professional athlete. I don't run a hundred miles a week. I don't win races.

But I'm fascinated by the stories hiding behind the people who make things happen.

My name is Corentin. I work in communications, I take photos, and I run — not fast, not far, but regularly. One day I realized there was a common thread running through all of it: telling the stories that bring people together.

Entre 2 sentiers was born from that realization, and the name isn't just a play on words. I'm between two professional projects, between communications and the outdoors, between marketing and photography, between the city and the mountains. And I believe the most interesting subjects live exactly there: between two paths, between two ways of seeing things. Not in the camp that's right, but in the tension that forces you to think.

This project is a space where I talk about outdoor sport: trail running mainly, but also cycling, hiking, climbing, and above all everything that happens behind the outdoors: marketing, territories, events, photography, the environment, and the people who make this world what it is.

My method comes down to three words: observe a fact, a news item, a trend; understand what it reveals, with the eye of a communications professional as much as an outdoor enthusiast; then question, rather than settle. An outdoor event can be both an economic opportunity and a pressure on the territory it crosses. A brand can sell dreams and greenwashing in the same campaign. I'd rather show that tension than pick a side for you.

The most fascinating stories in the outdoors aren't the ones that pit two camps against each other. They're the ones that force us to think together.

What this project isn't: another trail-running news feed, a motivation account, or a media outlet that makes it seem like the normal way to practice the outdoors is running a hundred miles a week. Most people who practice are amateurs — myself included. That reality has its full place here.

My goal isn't to have a hundred thousand followers. My goal is for someone, somewhere in an outdoor brand or a communications agency, to read these lines and think: "this person gets our world." That's why I favor consistency and regularity over virality.
